# Stage 10–15: Compute allocation and transfer

**Evidence level:** Historical report; not re-executed during this publication.

These stages charged online measurement cost and moved from condition-level to
instance-level revision scheduling. Costed conductance did not establish an
incremental scheduling benefit. A small calibration set of realized gains was
also insufficient in the half-plane transfer.

Local-convergence stopping was worse than the same-compute fixed-depth convex
hull by about 2.215 percentage points. Reachability was an explanatory mechanism,
not an optimal scheduler. The choice of which instance to revise mattered more
than an average condition-level priority.

Within the same noise regime, an observation-plus-R1 nonlinear benefit predictor
improved over uncertainty scheduling by about 2.323 percentage points on held-out
seed/dilation blocks. A Ridge predictor later failed at out-of-support noise.

**Correction:** Stage 16–20 showed that the Ridge result was not evidence of
universal failure under that noise shift: nonlinear predictors recovered gains.
Offline predictor training cost was not charged as online neural evaluation
cost; this limits the practical scheduling claim.
